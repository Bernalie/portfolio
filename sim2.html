Wait—there's no traditional plot?!

Good news: The Anthropocene Reviewed is a collection of 44 short personal essays (not a long, boring novel!). Author John Green takes random everyday things from our human-centered era (the "Anthropocene")—like Diet Dr Pepper, Canada geese, sunsets, and Mario Kart—and reviews them on a 5-star scale.

Quick Facts for Class

  • 1
    What is "Anthropocene"? The current geological age where humans are the #1 force reshaping Earth.
  • 2
    The Real "Plot": John's personal emotional arc—moving from isolation, OCD, and pandemic despair toward hope and human connection.
  • 3
    The Hook: Every essay starts with fun trivia/history, then secretly turns into a deeply relatable story about life.

The Hidden "Plot" Arc (Say This in Class Discussions!)

Part 1

Wonder & Weirdness

John starts by examining strange human inventions and natural wonders (like Halley's Comet and Staphylococcus aureus). He establishes that humans are both wildly destructive and deeply curious.

Part 2

Anxiety, Grief & COVID-19

As the book progresses, the essays get more personal. John opens up about his severe OCD, depression, losing loved ones, and writing during the 2020 pandemic lockdown when human connection felt impossible.

Part 3

Falling in Love with the World

By the final essays (like Auld Lang Syne and Three Farmers on Their Way to a Dance), John realizes that paying close attention to small, imperfect things is how we survive and love being alive.
